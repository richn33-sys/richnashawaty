# Local SEO Audit & Optimization Checklist

**A repeatable system for trades businesses (HVAC, plumbing, landscaping, auto repair, etc.)**
Internal working doc / client-facing service template. Strip or keep the "why" notes depending on audience.

---

## How to use this

This is a phased audit, not a one-time blast. Each phase produces a deliverable (usually a comparison spreadsheet) and a prioritized action list. The pattern throughout: **pull competitor data → find the gaps → act on the gaps**. The comparison-spreadsheet format is what makes it feel rigorous to a client, so lead with it.

Before starting, capture the client's business context once (name, address, phone, GBP URL, primary + secondary services, service-area cities, top target keywords, 3 named competitors with GBP URLs, current review count/rating, biggest current problem). Everything downstream references this.

A realistic cadence is one phase per 1–2 weeks. Don't promise specific ranking jumps on a timeline — present these as "high-confidence improvements" without dates.

## Phase 1 — Google Business Profile (highest immediate impact)

### 1. Category audit
Pull the primary + all secondary categories for the 3 competitors ranking in the Map Pack for the client's top keywords. Compare against the client's categories.
- **Deliverable:** one spreadsheet tab per keyword — business name, primary category, secondary categories, rating, review count, position.
- **Action:** list missing categories ranked by how many competitors share them. Categories all 3 share = add immediately; categories only 1 has = differentiation.
- **Why it works:** categories control which searches trigger the listing. A wrong/missing primary category caps everything else. Genuinely one of the fastest local wins.

### 2. Attributes audit
Extract every visible attribute (veteran-owned, free estimates, online appointments, 24/7, etc.) for client vs. 3 competitors.
- **Deliverable:** yes/no grid across all four listings.
- **Action:** three tiers — all competitors have it (table stakes), 2 of 3 (strong), 1 of 3 (differentiation).
- **Why it works:** affects both eligibility for specific searches and click-through (trust signals before the click).

### 3. Review velocity teardown
For each competitor, read the last ~50 reviews and pull: total count, rating, reviews in last 30/60/90 days, most-mentioned services, most-mentioned neighborhoods, recurring complaints, and the phrases customers naturally use.
- **Deliverable:** velocity comparison + a "keywords to ask customers to mention" list.
- **Why it works:** **velocity beats raw count.** A listing earning 15/month is stronger than one sitting on 200 old reviews. The review-phrase list becomes the client's review-request script.

### 4. Review response system
Audit response rate, speed, whether responses mention services/locations, and tone for client vs. competitors. Then build response templates (3 variations each for 5/4/3/1–2-star) at 40–80 words that naturally include a service + city, and read as human.
- **Why it works:** responses are keyword-rich content the client controls, and Google has indicated responding helps. A template library makes consistent responding realistic.

### 5. GBP posts strategy
Pull competitor posting frequency, types, image/CTA usage, and topics over 90 days. Build an 8-week calendar (2–3 posts/week) mixing seasonal promos, before/afters, neighborhood-specific posts, review highlights, and educational posts. Each post 100–150 words, one target keyword, clear CTA, plus a photo description.
- **Why it works:** most competitors don't post consistently, so this is usually a fast differentiator. Neighborhood-specific posts build location relevance.

### 6. Services section optimization
Extract competitors' full services list + descriptions. Cross-reference the client's website services against their GBP services to catch anything on the site but missing from GBP. Write 40–60-word descriptions for each service (primary keyword + one service area + a concrete benefit).
- **Why it works:** this section is fully client-controlled copy. The site-vs-GBP cross-reference catches a common gap (new service added to site, never added to GBP = invisible in Map Pack for it).

### 7. GBP description optimization
Compare competitor descriptions (keywords used, service areas, trust signals, CTA, tone). Write 2–3 versions under 750 chars — one keyword-focused, one conversion-focused, one trust-focused — to test sequentially.
- **Why it works:** treating it as a testable asset (run one ~30 days, check impressions/calls, swap) is an edge most don't bother with.

### 8. Photo audit
Compare photo count, recency, types, and quality. Build an 8-week upload plan beating the top competitor's velocity, with a weekly shot list, a keyword/location naming convention, and geotagging to served neighborhoods.
- **Why it works:** consistency signals an active business. Naming + geotagging are genuinely underused.
- **Caution:** skip the often-cited "42% more direction requests" stat — it's old and poorly sourced. Sell the tactic, not the number.

## Phase 2 — Website

### 9. Keyword gap audit *(needs SEMrush/Ahrefs)*
Find keywords where competitors rank top-20 and the client doesn't. Filter to local intent: ~100–2,000 volume, contains city/service/"near me"/"emergency"/"best"/"local", difficulty under ~40.
- **Deliverable:** top 20 by opportunity score, each tagged "optimize existing page" or "create new page."

### 10. Money-page audit *(needs Google Search Console)*
Export 3 months of queries + pages. Identify: pages in positions 4–15 (one push from top 3), high-impression/low-click pages (title + meta problem), zero-ranking pages, and cannibalization (right keyword, wrong page). Output quick wins / medium / long-term with exact on-page changes.

### 11. Service + city page builder
For each service-city combo the client lacks, build a full page: title (<60 char), meta (<155 char), H1, pain-point intro, local "why choose us," service details, social-proof placeholder, 3 city-specific FAQs, CTA. Plus URL slug + internal-link + citation suggestions per page.
- **Why it works:** Google ranks pages, not sites. No page for [service] in [city] = no ranking for it. This builds the location stack fast.

### 12. GSC page-2 sprint *(needs GSC)*
Find every keyword in positions 11–20 with 100+ monthly impressions. For each, check keyword presence in title/H1/first-100-words, word count, internal links, current meta. Build a 30-day sprint and **write the actual new title/H1/meta copy**, not instructions.
- **Why it works:** page-2 keywords are the single highest-ROI tactic here. Demand already exists; you're one push from visibility.

### 13. Review sentiment → copy
Read ~100 competitor reviews; extract emotional words, specific outcomes, pre-service fears, and recommendation phrases. Compare to the client's reviews to find gaps, then rewrite GBP description, homepage headline/subhead, review-request script, and 3 social-proof statements in customers' actual language.
- **Why it works:** writing copy in customers' words is real CRO. (It's standard practice despite being framed as secret — still worth doing.)

## Phase 3 — Backlinks & Authority

### 14. Competitor backlink audit *(needs Ahrefs)*
Export competitors' dofollow links (DR 20+, real traffic, non-sitewide). Find domains linking to all 3 / 2 / 1 competitors but not the client. For high-priority targets: domain type, how the link was earned, realistic chance, outreach approach. Build a 90-day plan (easy citations → local news/sponsors → authority publications) with ready-to-send outreach emails.
- **Why it works:** a few real contextual links beat dozens of random directory submissions.

### 15. Citation / NAP audit
Search the business across GBP, Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps, Facebook, BBB, Angi, Houzz, Yellow Pages, etc. Record exact name/address/phone/URL per platform, flag every inconsistency and duplicate. Deliver a priority fix list + missing high-value directories + a maintenance checklist.
- **Why it works:** NAP inconsistency suppresses rankings, and fixes often show results within ~30 days. Bread-and-butter.

### 16. Search-intent mapping *(needs SEMrush)*
Pull niche keywords (20+ volume) and bucket into 4 stages: problem-unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → ready-to-hire. Map stage-4 to service pages/GBP, stage-3 to comparison/FAQ, stage-2 to educational blog, stage-1 to early-trust content. Identify 5 stage-4 keywords to prioritize.
- **Why it works:** stops the common mistake of over-investing in high-volume awareness terms that don't convert. Stage-4 converts far better.

## Phase 4 — Content & Tracking

### 17. Content gap analysis *(needs SEMrush)*
Find keywords competitors have content for and the client doesn't. Filter to 50–500 volume, question words, problem-related. Top 20 organized into problem-awareness / solution-comparison / local-service, each with title, slug, and a 200-word brief (keywords, questions to answer, word count, internal links, CTA). Prioritize problem-awareness content.

### 18. Entity / schema optimization *(lower priority for small trades)*
Check for an existing knowledge panel and Wikidata entry, audit current schema (rich-results test), check brand-name consistency. Build a plan: complete LocalBusiness JSON-LD to paste, presence on Crunchbase/LinkedIn/associations, consistent brand mentions.
- **Reality check:** oversold as a "moat." For an HVAC company, Wikipedia eligibility and a knowledge panel are mostly out of reach. **The schema (JSON-LD) is the part that actually does work** — treat the rest as nice-to-have, not a priority.

### 19. Competitor posting-pattern analysis
Forensic pull of competitor GBP posting history (date, day, time, type, length, image, CTA, topic, neighborhood, offer). Find day/time/seasonal patterns and posting gaps to exploit, then build a market-specific cadence + 4 weeks of posts.
- **Note:** overlaps heavily with #5 — run only if the client is already posting and wants to optimize, otherwise #5 is enough.

### 20. Monthly performance report *(needs GSC + GBP insights + GA4)*
30-day vs. prior-30 across GSC (clicks, impressions, CTR, position, keyword movers, page movers), GBP (views, branded vs. discovery, calls, directions, website clicks, review change), and GA4 (organic sessions, conversion rate, landing pages, bounce). Deliver a one-page report: 3 wins, 3 problems, the single most important next action, and whether GBP calls went up or down.
- **Why it works:** keeps reporting on calls/revenue, not vanity traffic. This is also a natural retainer deliverable.

## Operational cautions (for whoever runs this)

- **Tool access:** Phases 9–10, 12, 14, 16–17, 20 require logging into SEMrush / Ahrefs / GSC / GA4. Use the client's own accounts with permission; don't share credentials in chat.
- **Scraping at volume:** Repeated automated GBP/Maps scraping gets flaky and can hit rate limits or ToS issues on the paid tools. Pull deliberately, not in bulk loops.
- **No timeline promises:** Present results as high-confidence improvements without committing to "X days." Local ranking timelines vary too much.
- **Drop the dubious stat** (the 42% photo figure) before anything goes client-facing.
