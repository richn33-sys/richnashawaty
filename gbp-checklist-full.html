The Boston Google Business Profile Checklist

26 items across 6 categories — the same checklist I work through when I optimize a Boston business's Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business). Built for this market: neighborhood naming, review-velocity benchmarks, and the category audit that decides whether you make the local pack.

26 items 6 categories Updated June 2026 Interactive — click to check off

How to use this checklist

Work through the categories in order — Foundation first, since you can't optimize a profile you haven't claimed and completed. Priority labels (HIGH / MED / LOW) tell you what to fix first when time is short. Click any item to check it off — your progress is saved in your browser. When you're done, the whole thing takes most businesses a focused afternoon.

01 Foundation — claim, verify, complete 8 items
Claim and verify your profile
Check business.google.com. Video verification is now the default — record one continuous take showing signage, interior, and a live management action. Postcard verification is a 5–14 day fallback.
HIGH
Business name — exact match to real-world branding
Must match your website, signage, and directories exactly. Do not add keywords or neighborhoods ("Best Plumber Boston") — that's a direct path to suspension.
HIGH
Address and service area configured correctly
If you go to customers, hide your address and set a service area by city/zip. If they come to you, show your address. Don't set a service area far beyond where you actually work.
HIGH
Phone number matches website and directories
Use a local Boston number (617, 781, 857) rather than an 800 number. Local numbers correlate with better local pack performance and signal you're actually local.
MED
Website URL points to the most relevant page
Don't default to the homepage. A plumber should point to the plumbing services page — reinforce the primary category and service area, don't make visitors hunt.
MED
Hours accurate and complete — including special hours
Set holiday hours in advance to avoid the "Hours may differ" warning that cuts click-through. Note Boston-specific dates (Patriot's Day, Evacuation Day) if you observe them.
MED
Description — ~750 characters, conversational, no keyword stuffing
Lead with what you do and who you serve. Mention your service area naturally. Google's AI reads descriptions for entity context, not keyword density — and a good one feeds AI Overview appearances.
MED
All attributes and amenities completed
Accessibility, payment methods, appointment availability, women-/veteran-owned designations, service guarantees. These feed Google's structured data parser and can surface in AI answers.
LOW
02 Category selection — the highest-leverage edit 3 items
Primary category — the most specific accurate match
The single most impactful field. "Emergency Plumber" ranks differently than "Plumber"; "Italian Restaurant" than "Restaurant." Switching it can move visibility more than 20 reviews. Audit competitors first.
HIGH
Secondary categories — only for services you actively offer
Each legitimate secondary category expands the queries you're eligible for. Don't add categories for services you don't offer — category stuffing risks suspension.
MED
Run a competitor category audit before setting yours
Search your top 3–5 target queries in an incognito Google Maps window. Record the primary category of the top 3 results for each — that's what Google is rewarding, and what yours should align to.
HIGH
03 Boston-specific signals most guides miss 3 items
Use Boston neighborhood naming conventions Google has learned
Use the formal names in fields and service descriptions: South End (not South Boston — different neighborhood), East Boston (not "Eastie"), Dorchester (not "Dot"), Jamaica Plain (not "JP"). These distinctions matter to residents searching — and Google has learned them.
HIGH
Call out the Route 128 / I-495 corridor towns by name
If you serve Waltham, Newton, Needham, Natick, or other corridor towns, name them explicitly in your description and services ("serving Newton, Waltham, and Needham"). A service area that technically covers them but never mentions them leaves suburban-query relevance on the table.
MED
Note MBTA / T-stop proximity where customers come to you
For restaurants, retail, studios, and personal services, "one block from Kenmore Station" or "steps from the Green Line" is a legitimate relevance signal that matches how residents actually search. Work it into the description naturally, not as a keyword.
MED
04 Reviews — velocity over volume 4 items

The most common GBP mistake Boston businesses make is treating reviews as a one-time campaign — 30 in a week, then silence. A consistent flow over 90 days outranks a burst followed by nothing. Google rewards businesses that look actively in business. Here's roughly where the bar sits by market:

Market / Neighborhood Competitive threshold Monthly velocity
Back Bay, Downtown, Seaport60–100+ reviews3–5 / month
South End, Beacon Hill, Cambridge40–70 reviews2–4 / month
Jamaica Plain, Somerville, Brookline25–50 reviews2–3 / month
Dorchester, Roxbury, East Boston15–30 reviews1–2 / month
Route 128 suburbs (Newton, Waltham, Needham)20–40 reviews2–3 / month
I-495 corridor (Marlborough, Framingham, Natick)15–25 reviews1–2 / month
Build a repeatable review request system
The businesses with the most reviews have the most consistent ask, not the most customers. A text or email with your Google review link, sent within 24 hours of completion, converts at 10–20% for most service businesses.
HIGH
Respond to every review — positive and negative — within 48 hours
Responses signal business engagement. Brief, specific thanks for positives; acknowledge and offer to resolve offline for negatives. Prospects read your responses as much as the reviews.
MED
Never buy or incentivize reviews
Even "leave a review for 10% off" violates Google's policy and risks suspension. Google's review detection is sophisticated, and a Boston-market suspension takes months to recover from.
HIGH
Monitor and flag fake negative reviews weekly
Competitors in competitive Boston categories occasionally plant fake negatives. If a review has no reviewer history, no other local reviews, and matches no real interaction, flag it. Removal isn't always fast, but documentation matters.
LOW
05 Posts & photos — conversion assets, not ranking signals 4 items
Post at least once per week
One post per week is the minimum to maintain an active freshness signal. Posts don't directly move local pack position, but they lift click-through and feed Google's AI summaries. Every post should include a photo and a clear call to action.
HIGH
Cover photo — ~1080x608px, branded, not stock
Your cover anchors the profile header. Use your storefront, team, or a finished project — not generic stock. Set one explicitly so Google doesn't auto-rotate a customer upload in its place.
MED
Minimum 15 photos — then add 2–5 per month
Profiles with photos get more direction and website-click requests. Start with 15 strong shots (exterior, interior, team, work samples), then add a few each month — the steady addition signals an active business.
MED
Seed the Q&A section with your most common questions
Anyone with a Google account can answer questions about your business — including competitors and well-meaning customers with wrong info. Add the 5–10 questions customers ask most, and answer them yourself first.
LOW
06 Schema & website alignment 4 items
LocalBusiness schema on homepage and contact page
Populate name, address, telephone, openingHours, geo (lat/long), url, image, priceRange, and areaServed to exactly match your GBP. The exact match is what Google uses to verify your business entity.
HIGH
NAP visible site-wide — header or footer
Name, Address, and Phone on every page, typically the footer, in a format identical to your GBP and directories. Even "St." vs "Street" creates entity confusion that weakens local rankings.
MED
Embedded Google Map on your contact page
Embedding your map creates an explicit link between your website and your GBP listing. Minor signal, but free, takes 5 minutes, and reinforces the connection.
LOW
Citation consistency across major directories
Check Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps, Yellow Pages, and any industry directory (Angi, Houzz, Avvo, Healthgrades). Name, address, and phone must be identical everywhere — inconsistency weakens entity trust and rankings.
MED
