What local SEO actually includes

Before evaluating price, it's worth being clear on what local SEO is and isn't. A lot of confusion about pricing comes from comparing apples to oranges — one consultant's "local SEO" might mean GBP optimization and citation cleanup, while another's includes landing pages, link building, and monthly reporting.

Core local SEO typically covers:

  • Google Business Profile optimization — categories, attributes, services, description, photos, review strategy
  • Citation/NAP consistency — your name, address, and phone matching across all major directories
  • On-page local signals — neighborhood mentions, local schema markup, location-specific content
  • Local keyword research — what your customers actually search for in the Boston area
  • Review management — strategy for generating and responding to reviews
  • Monthly reporting — GBP insights, call volume, ranking changes

More comprehensive engagements add local landing pages (one per neighborhood or city served), local link building, competitor analysis, and content strategy. These add meaningful cost and meaningful results — but they're not necessary for every business at every stage.

What drives the cost up

Two engagements quoted at the same monthly rate can involve very different amounts of work. Here's what actually drives local SEO cost:

Market competitiveness

Personal injury law in Boston is a completely different competitive landscape than residential cleaning services in Lexington. The more competitive your market, the more ongoing work it takes to maintain and improve rankings — and the higher the monthly cost should be to reflect that reality. A quote that doesn't account for your competitive landscape isn't based on your situation.

Number of locations or service areas

One location in Cambridge is a fundamentally different scope than five locations across Greater Boston. Each location needs its own GBP, its own citation presence, and its own local content. Multi-location work is legitimate reason for higher pricing.

Current state of your online presence

A business with a clean GBP, consistent citations, and a solid website needs maintenance and growth work. A business with an unclaimed GBP, conflicting NAP data across 20 directories, and a ten-year-old website needs remediation work first. The starting point matters significantly for both scope and timeline.

Whether content is included

Local landing pages and blog content cost real time to produce well. An engagement that includes monthly content creation will cost more than one that doesn't — and should, because content is where much of the long-term ranking value comes from.

Solo consultant vs agency — what you actually get

Solo consultant
  • You work with the same person throughout — no handoffs
  • Senior expertise on your account from day one
  • Lower overhead = more of your budget goes to actual work
  • Direct accountability — one person owns your results
  • Typically starting at $750/month for a strong Boston specialist
  • Limited capacity — may not scale to complex multi-location needs
Local agency
  • More capacity — can handle broader scope simultaneously
  • Team specialization — dedicated writer, analyst, strategist
  • Higher overhead = more of your budget goes to infrastructure
  • Risk of junior staff on your account despite senior pitch
  • Typically $2,000–$5,000/month for a credible Boston agency
  • Better suited for multi-location or high-volume needs

For most Boston small businesses with a single location, the solo consultant model delivers better value. You get senior expertise, direct accountability, and lower overhead — which means more actual work for your budget. The agency model makes sense when you need the capacity a team provides, not just because you assume bigger means better.

The Boston market reality

Boston has a high concentration of SEO agencies serving the same local business market. That competition keeps pricing relatively honest, but it also means the market includes a wide range in quality. The credential to look for isn't the agency's size — it's the specific experience of the person who will be doing your work. Ask directly: who handles my account day-to-day, and what is their background?

How to evaluate whether you're getting value

Local SEO is notorious for being hard to evaluate because results take time and attribution is imperfect. Here's what to track:

GBP calls and direction requests

GBP Insights shows how many people called your business or requested directions from your listing each month. This is the most direct measure of local SEO performance — it's actual customer actions, not rankings. If this number isn't growing over a 3–6 month engagement, something isn't working.

Ranking positions for local keywords

Track a set of 5–10 target local searches monthly — "HVAC Cambridge," "plumber Back Bay," your primary service plus your primary neighborhoods. If positions aren't improving over 3–6 months of paid engagement, that's meaningful data.

Map pack appearances

For local service businesses, appearing in the Google local pack (the three business listings with the map) for relevant searches is the highest-value SEO outcome. Track which searches trigger your listing in the pack and watch for expansion over time.

The question to ask monthly

A good local SEO consultant should be able to answer this every month: What did we do, what did it change, and what are we doing next? If you can't get a specific answer to that question, you're not getting the transparency you're paying for.
